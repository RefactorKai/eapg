\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

TrendProf fits growth models to basic-block execution counts across workloads and ranks program locations by scaling behavior~\cite{goldsmith2007measuring}, later extended to loop and call contexts~\cite{goldsmith2009measuring}. Perf-Taint combines static pruning with dynamic taint analysis for Extra-P to isolate performance-relevant parameters from uninvolved code~\cite{copik2021extrap}. Algorithmic Profiling infers empirical cost functions from observed inputs and algorithmic steps~\cite{zaparanuks2012algorithmic}, while Cody-Kenny et al. use mutation analysis to find low-frequency statements whose edits alter total cost~\cite{codykenny2018performance}. EAPG takes dynamic counts and static work proxies from one program version and applies scoped def/use rules without executing candidate edits during prediction.

Program slicing extracts statements that can influence a chosen criterion~\cite{weiser1981slicing}, typically over program dependence graphs combining control and data dependences~\cite{ferrante1987program}. Soremekun et al. compare dynamic slicing and statistical localization for functional faults~\cite{soremekun2021locating}. Rather than building full backward closures, EAPG restricts selection using AST parent scopes and variable definitions/uses around a growth symptom's guards; our prospective design isolates this scope restriction against a conventional CFG closure under shared symptoms and ranking rules.

Static analyzers such as CLARITY detect redundant collection traversals in Java~\cite{olivo2015static}, whereas ScaleLens combines static and dynamic profiling to diagnose scalability faults in distributed systems~\cite{zhu2026understanding}. EffiHolmes pairs differential profiling with LLM reasoning to rank fix locations in Python repositories~\cite{yang2026effiholmes}. EffiLearner~\cite{huang2024effilearner} and ECCO~\cite{waghjale2024ecco} feed profiling hotspots or execution histories back into code-editing LLMs to improve efficiency while preserving correctness; we test analogous raw-code and hotspot-annotated baselines (\texttt{raw\_code}, \texttt{hotspot\_raw}) alongside EAPG graphs on fixed node localization.

Large efficiency benchmarks such as PIE~\cite{shypula2024pie}, UOJ-Bench~\cite{xu2026uoj}, and CppPerf-DB~\cite{ho2026cppperf} collect real submission or patch pairs, yet real-world C++ repositories and competitive-programming submissions routinely rely on non-standard headers, macros, namespaces, and containers. Our source-domain audits evaluate what fraction of published corpora and OEIS scalar tasks~\cite{oeis2026} actually enter a verified single-function scalar slice comparison without semantic gaps.
