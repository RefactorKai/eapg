\begin{abstract}
A program statement whose execution dominates runtime can lie downstream of the edit causing its finite-range growth. Execution-annotated program graphs (EAPG) pair hit counts with operation-work proxies and use scoped definitions and uses to nominate candidate edit locations. Across 100 constructed C++17 pairs, work profiles emitted 100 symptoms versus 80 for hit-only profiles, and scoped slicing retained all 100 mechanism references while shrinking candidate sets by 89.0\%. Under a strict 13-group split across six prompt representations, QLoRA-adapted \texttt{Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B} reached 56/66 (79.77\% equal-group) multi-candidate Top-1 with work-annotated \nolinkurl{abstract_graph}, versus 44/66 without work exponents (\nolinkurl{abstract_hits_only}), 41/66 with code previews added (\nolinkurl{full_eapg}; \nolinkurl{abstract_graph} minus \nolinkurl{full_eapg} group-weighted difference $+34.02$ pp, 95\% interval $[+6.32, +62.12]$), and 36/66 for the unbiased symbolic rule. In a bounded feasibility execution of our scalar protocol across 10 unseen families (80-family frame with 280 placeholder-quarantined slots; below the 60-family confirmatory threshold), \texttt{work\_scoped} (\nolinkurl{def_use=False}) scored 15/20 (75.00\%) vs.\ 14/20 for pilot-refined \nolinkurl{work_scoped_symbolic} and 0/20 for \texttt{work\_conventional} ($+75.00$ pp $[+60.00,+90.00]$, driven by shared ranker hoisting of CFG initializers) alongside alerts on 5/5 quadratic and 0/5 linear shams (5/10 shams pooled) and 10/10 appropriate controls, while live 4-bit inference on the 20 core positives showed fold-0 \nolinkurl{hotspot_raw} (13/20, 65.00\%) out-transferring \nolinkurl{abstract_graph} (11/20, 55.00\%; below base 12/20). Blinded three-agent adjudication over 14 positive (11 groups, below the 20-group confirmatory threshold) and two negative controls replicated stress profiles and showed strictly out-of-fold \nolinkurl{abstract_graph} at 10/14 (63.64\%)—above unbiased \nolinkurl{def_use=False} symbolic (6/14, 54.55\%; $+9.09$ pp), matching pilot-refined \nolinkurl{def_use=True} symbolic (10/14), and below partially in-fold \texttt{fold-0} (12/14, 81.82\%).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A repeated traversal can dominate an execution profile even when a cheap state reset causes the repeated work. Inspecting only the expensive loop then directs attention to a symptom. A second problem arises when a statement is executed once per iteration but its cost rises with the size of a container. For example, counting calls to a search routine measures how often it is invoked. The number of elements inspected can grow on a different scale.

EAPG uses a sparse set of source-level macro-nodes to connect those two observations. It estimates finite-range growth from hit counts and operation-specific work proxies. Starting from a selected symptom, it then considers scoped writes to variables that govern the symptom. A ranker can order the resulting candidates. This workflow uses one evaluated program and a registered input family. The study uses paired programs or reversible interventions to establish reference evidence outside the prediction path.

We ask three empirical questions across five evidence strata (13-group pilot cross-validation, locked counting calibration across eight library conditions, authored executed/analytic probes and source-intake screens, an executed 10-family prospective scalar cohort, and a blinded 16-pair external contract evaluation). \textbf{RQ1} asks how work weighting, scoped candidate selection, prompt serialization (six source, hotspot, static, hits-only, abstract-graph, and full representations), and decoding constraints change symptom coverage, candidate reduction, and Top-1 agreement on grouped cross-validation and unseen scalar templates. \textbf{RQ2} asks where scoped slicing improves upon or fails conventional CFG backward slicing under shared symptoms and rankers, and what fraction of public C++ repair pairs and scalar tasks enters their shared domain. \textbf{RQ3} asks how closely container-size proxies match counted operations across optimization flags, how operation growth differs from wall-clock timing on human code, and how blinded multi-agent adjudication and strictly out-of-fold evaluation behave under \texttt{eapg-external-predictions-v1}.

The contributions are: (i) the EAPG macro-node profiler and scoped def/use slicing formulation with exact scaling, rank-hoisting, and selection criteria; (ii) a full representation, slice-masking, and symbolic-tuning ablation on the grouped synthetic pilot alongside live out-of-distribution tests on 10 unseen scalar families showing when \nolinkurl{hotspot_raw} out-transfers \nolinkurl{abstract_graph}; (iii) executable counterexamples and feasibility bounds covering raw-AST comparator blockers (0/714 UOJ, 0/100 pilot), a 10-group OEIS scalar-intake funnel (5/11 eligible), label non-identifiability under comparator swaps, and transitive vs.\ overwritten-write pruning bounds; and (iv) a hash-pinned multi-agent feasibility execution of the prospective scalar cohort ($10 < 60$ families, 15/20 vs.\ 0/20 CFG under shared \texttt{STATE\_UPD} hoisting) and blinded three-agent external contract (\texttt{eapg-external-predictions-v1}, $14+2$ pairs across $11 < 20$ groups, contrasting strict OOF \nolinkurl{abstract_graph} against both \nolinkurl{def_use=False/True} symbolic baselines and in-fold inflation).

\section{Interpretation and validity}
\label{sec:validity}

\paragraph{From expensive operation to edit location}
Agreement with a mechanism-informed edit reference establishes predictive agreement on the pilot, where the reference heuristic saw both versions and mechanism scope. On prospective tasks, reversible single-site interventions isolate the edit's effect on counted operations and stdout equivalence across tested inputs. Prior fault-localization studies note the fragility of equating patch locations with unique root causes~\cite{soremekun2023evaluating}. Our metric measures localization of a specified edit site; the prospective protocol preserves unresolved mappings and precommitted equivalent spans.

\paragraph{Interpretation of a slow-side alert and AST approximations}
An appropriate superlinear program can emit a work symptom. In the comparator-swap diagnostic, identical evaluated sources and traces carry verified opposite labels across two comparators, so a predictor reading only the evaluated program and trace cannot distinguish the two labels. Sensitivity and false-alert rates therefore depend on declared comparators and controls. Additionally, the macro-node extractor places all identifiers in a \texttt{for}-loop update expression into both $\mathrm{defs}$ and $\mathrm{uses}$ (\texttt{pipeline.py:312--314}); this affects 0/193 \texttt{for} loops in the 100 pilot slow programs yet mislabels read-only step/block variables as loop definitions in 3/7 syntax-passing OEIS cases (\texttt{A001399} \texttt{b += jump}, \texttt{A006218\_step} \texttt{j += step}, \texttt{A006218\_quotient} \texttt{l = r + 1}), and the human-written PIE pair separates counted quadratic inner traversals from process-level timing slopes that fell below the 0.50 threshold.

\paragraph{Selection, representation, and tuning leakage}
The grouped refit exposes eight of nine mechanisms in single folds, with 34 singleton slices that force agreement under constrained decoding~\cite{bates2024cross}. Furthermore, the pilot-refined symbolic rule (\nolinkurl{use_loop_def_use=True}) adds 26 hits in \texttt{B.1} and \texttt{B.2} from pilot loop-bound misses, whereas prospective arms fix \nolinkurl{use_loop_def_use=False}. Comparing \nolinkurl{SLICE}-tagged vs.\ untagged prompts and constrained decoding separates work features from pruning hints, though public C++ code may appear in base pretraining corpora. For prospective inference, $|V| \le 26$ (\texttt{NODE\_LIMIT}) and prompt length $+1 \le 512$ tokens (\texttt{CONTEXT\_LIMIT}) act as explicit eligibility filters recorded in $D_{\mathrm{pos}}$ vs.\ $D_{\mathrm{elig}}$.

\paragraph{Language, intake attrition, and external blockers}
Both slicers cover restricted C++17 subsets, and the conventional comparator requires single-\texttt{main()} integral scalar code (0/714 UOJ and 0/100 pilot raw-AST pass rate). Even inside the scalar domain, OEIS intake exposed mechanism collapse (\texttt{A000328} and \texttt{A000201} share cursor resets), rejection of genuine 0.50-order speedups below the 0.55 finite-range slope floor (\texttt{A006218\_quotient}, \texttt{A002088}), and an immediate-parent-scope miss (\texttt{A400004}). Library calibration isolates operation counts from compiler optimizations (\texttt{-O0/-O2/-O3}) but tests only fixed full-vector calls.

\paragraph{Host execution, subagent custody, and remaining external scope}
Three scope conditions characterize the feasibility studies in Section~\ref{sec:prospective-multiagent}: (1) both Part~A ($10 < 60$ families, \texttt{primary\_sized=false}) and Part~B ($11 < 20$ groups, \texttt{positive\_transfer\_evidence=false}) fall below confirmatory sample-size gates, with Part~A's 280 quarantined slots built as placeholder families to validate end-to-end quarantine mechanics and Part~A emitting alerts on 5/5 quadratic and 0/5 linear shams (5/10 shams pooled) and 10/10 appropriate controls; (2) Part~A CPU execution (378 builds, 6,804 oracle runs, 738 profiles) matched byte-for-byte between unmodified Linux WSL2 Bubblewrap~0.11.1 (\nolinkurl{systemd-run}, GCC~15.2.0, FD~199 counter transport, all Gate~1 probes passing) and Windows~11 MSYS2 GCC~15.1.0 (\nolinkurl{score.json} \texttt{ce049b32}), whereas Windows Part~B stress re-execution under \texttt{2c95df0e} matched archived Linux \texttt{c4260272} stress profiles and live 4-bit inference ran on the Windows RTX~4060 GPU; and (3) verification and adjudication used four isolated subagents rather than human contributors, while the full PIE test split remains sealed (Part~B's 14 positives comprise 12 out-of-fold pilot pairs, 1 authored smoke pair, and \texttt{PIE\_DEV\_p03497} whose wall-clock slope fell below 0.50).

\section{Conclusion}

On the constructed pilot, work weighting increased symptom coverage from 80 to 100 pairs and scoped selection reduced candidate sets by 89.0\%. Across the 13-group ablation, the under-sized 10-family scalar cohort, and blinded three-agent external adjudication, scoped slicing eliminates the CFG initializer/accumulator false-positive pool that drives \texttt{work\_conventional} to 0/20 under \texttt{STATE\_UPD} hoisting (15/20 vs.\ 0/20), while live 4-bit inference separates in-distribution graph adaptation (\nolinkurl{abstract_graph} 79.77\% multi-candidate) from unseen-template transfer where identifier-preserving \nolinkurl{hotspot_raw} leads (13/20 vs.\ 11/20 core) and strict OOF evaluation shows lower observed agreement than partially in-fold evaluation in this $n=14$ cohort (12/14 to 10/14, matching refined \nolinkurl{def_use=True} symbolic and beating unbiased \nolinkurl{def_use=False} at 6/14). Authored counterexamples, raw-AST blocker audits, and the OEIS screen delimit where two-scope and transitive chains break scoped slicing and define what powered human-authored deployment requires.
